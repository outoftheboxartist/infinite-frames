Build it, step by step

  1. Measure the box

    Measure the window opening inside the frame (width and height), then the depth available behind the front. Enter the opening as the window size. Choose a recess smaller than the depth so there's room for a light behind the picture.

  2. Pick the recess

    The recess sets how far you tilt for each pose: the readout above shows degrees per frame. About 3–6° per frame feels lively when people walk past. A bigger recess means less tilt per pose. Foam board is a handy spacer: 3/16 in (5 mm) or 1/8 in (3 mm).

  3. Print the test patches

    • Print Test patch, back on paper and Test patch, front on clear film, both at 100%.
    • Lay the film on the paper with a spacer the thickness of your recess under its edges, then slide it sideways until one clean butterfly shows.
    • If it looks muddy, lower the slits per inch. If the stripes are obvious, raise it.

  4. Print the back layer

    • Lit from the front: matte photo paper.
    • Lit from behind: print on transparency film or vellum so light passes through. A sheet of your prism film or a piece of white paper behind it spreads the light evenly.
    • Use the printer's best quality and let the ink dry fully.

  5. Print the front layer

    • Print on clear inkjet transparency film (rough, coated side) or laser film. The black must be solid: if light leaks through, print a second copy and stack them exactly.
    • Prism film bends light and blurs the slits, so it's a poor barrier. Use it as the diffuser behind the back layer instead.
    • No glass needed: the film can be held flat by the box's front frame.

  6. Assemble

    • Mount the back layer at the back of the box. If it's lit from behind, put the diffuser and light behind it.
    • Fit the spacer frame of your recess thickness all the way around.
    • Lay the front film on the spacer, then line up the corner marks on both layers.
    • Look straight at the box and slide the film a hair sideways until the whole window shows one clean pose. Then tape it down and close the frame.

  7. Light it

    Even, soft light matters more than bright light. An LED panel or strip behind a diffuser gives the cleanest look for a backlit box. Send me the light you have and I'll tune the back-layer settings for it.

  8. Tilt and enjoy

    At 0° the butterfly flaps when you turn the box side to side or walk past it. At 90°, tilt it forward and back instead. In multi-directional mode each zone has its own angle, so a turn wakes one zone and a tilt wakes another. Change angles or frame counts later and reprint just the two layers.
